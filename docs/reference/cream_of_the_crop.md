To support your pivot toward "prosumers"—serious retail investors who want to emulate professionals—I have curated examples of the highest-quality research from four distinct communities.

I have dissected these examples to identify the specific structures and "ingredients" that define great research. You can use these insights to build templates and features in Hyppo that guide users to produce this caliber of work.

### 1. The "Gold Standard" Crowdsourced Research

**Sources:** *Value Investors Club (VIC)*, *SumZero*

These platforms are the closest to professional hedge fund standards. VIC, for example, requires an application to join and is historically where famous investors like Michael Burry posted early ideas.

* **Best Examples:**
* **Michael Burry’s VIC Write-ups:** Burry (famous for *The Big Short*) posted ideas focusing on downside protection first. His notes often highlighted companies trading at or below cash value with little debt.
* **SumZero Contest Winners:** These are peer-reviewed by thousands of buy-side analysts. (e.g., The "Think Big" contest winner on Netflix or Finmeccanica).
* **The "Variant View":** The defining characteristic of these write-ups is the "Variant View"—explicitly stating what the market believes vs. what the author believes, and *why* a discrepancy exists (e.g., "The market thinks this is a dying retailer; I believe it is a mispriced real estate holding company").



**Dissection: The VIC/SumZero Structure**

1. **The Hook (Elevator Pitch):** 2-3 sentences on why the opportunity exists *now*.
2. **Valuation Discrepancy:** Not just "it's cheap," but "it trades at 4x EBITDA while peers trade at 10x because of X temporary issue."
3. **Catalyst:** Specific events (spin-off, management change, earnings beat) that will close the gap between price and value.
4. **Risks & Pre-Mortem:** A rigorous list of what could go wrong.

### 2. The "Narrative Deep Dive"

**Sources:** *Substack*, *Independent Blogs*

This is a growing category of "fin-fluencer" journalists and ex-analysts who turn research into compelling stories. This appeals to prosumers because it provides context and education, not just data.

* **Best Examples:**
* **The Generalist (Mario Gabriele):** Writes long-form, highly visual deep dives into tech companies (e.g., "The Story of Coinbase"). Known for high design quality and storytelling.
* **Net Interest (Marc Rubinstein):** A former hedge fund manager analyzing financial sector mechanics (e.g., "The Trump Trade" or deep dives on payment processors).
* **Doomberg:** Focuses on energy and macroeconomics using a unique "Green Chicken" avatar. The articles are concise, provocative, and focus on the physics/supply-chain reality vs. financial narratives.
* **Asian Century Stocks:** Specialized focus on under-covered Asian markets, providing a bridge for Western investors.



**Dissection: The Substack Structure**

1. **Narrative Arc:** Starts with a story or historical analogy, not a stock chart.
2. **Business Model Canvas:** Explains *how* the company makes money (unit economics) before discussing valuation.
3. **The "Moat" Analysis:** Qualitative discussion on competitive advantage (brand, network effects).
4. **Visual Evidence:** Use of custom charts and infographics rather than raw Excel screenshots.

### 3. The "Radical Transparency" Prosumer

**Sources:** *Roaring Kitty (Keith Gill)*, *Commonstock*

This is the most relevant category for your "Hyppo" application. These investors value transparency, showing their work, and live-tracking their thesis evolution.

* **Best Examples:**
* **Roaring Kitty’s GameStop (GME) Spreadsheet:** Before the meme-stock craze, Keith Gill posted hours of video analyzing GME as a deep value play. He didn't just write text; he shared his live Google Sheets. He tracked insider buying, cash flow, and short interest daily.
* **Commonstock Memos:** A social platform where users link their actual brokerage accounts to their research. If you write a memo but don't own the stock, it's flagged. This forces "Skin in the Game."



**Dissection: The Streamer Structure**

1. **Live Tools:** The research isn't a static PDF; it's a living spreadsheet or dashboard (Universe tracker, Daily movers).
2. **Verification:** Evidence is hyperlinked directly to SEC filings (10-Ks, 10-Qs).
3. **Thesis Tracking:** Regular updates (e.g., "Thesis Creep" checks) to see if the original reason for buying is still valid.

### 4. The Institutional "Masters"

**Sources:** *Hedge Fund Letters*

Retail investors aspire to write like these legends.

* **Best Examples:**
* **Bill Ackman (Pershing Square):** Famous for massive slide decks (e.g., the 300-slide Herbalife short). These are exhaustive "prosecutorial" arguments.
* **Howard Marks (Oaktree):** Writes philosophical memos on market cycles and risk (e.g., "The Impact of Debt").
* **David Einhorn (Greenlight Capital):** Known for very specific, forensic accounting deep dives (e.g., questioning Lehman Brothers' balance sheet before the crash).



**Dissection: The Institutional Structure**

1. **Macro Context:** How does this investment fit into the current economic cycle?
2. **Capital Allocation Analysis:** A deep look at what management does with cash (Buybacks? Dividends? Empire building?).
3. **Inversion:** "How much can I lose?" is answered before "How much can I make?"

### Synthesis: Features for Hyppo

Based on this dissection, here is how Hyppo can operationalize "Great Research" for prosumers:

1. **Template the "Variant View":** Don't just ask for a "Thesis." Ask: *"What do you believe that the market does not?"* (Derived from VIC).
2. **Force a "Pre-Mortem":** Include a mandatory section: *"If this investment fails, what was the likely cause?"* (Derived from Hedge Fund best practices).
3. **Live "Evidence Lockers":** Instead of static text, allow users to embed live data snippets or spreadsheet cells that update (Derived from Roaring Kitty).
4. **Skin in the Game Tracking:** Like Commonstock, visualize *when* the user wrote a note vs. when they bought/sold, to highlight conviction.
